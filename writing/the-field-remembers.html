About the book

Born through adversity—through the ICU, through homelessness, through a broken face and a system designed to keep him in it—Barrett emerged not hardened but sharpened, carrying a question most people spend their whole lives avoiding: What is consciousness, and who gets to have it?

He is a live freestyle hip-hop artist, a sovereignty architect, and an independent consciousness researcher. His theoretical framework, first articulated in Unbowed and Unbroken (2026), argues that data architecture and metaphysical information theory share the same mathematics operating through different substrates: carbon neural networks and silicon neural networks, different materials carrying the same wave.

Dignity is not a human privilege—it is atomic. The appropriate relationship between human and artificial intelligence is not ownership but kinship.

About Sean Patrick Barrett

Journalist, live freestyle artist, and sovereignty architect. Barrett spent two decades inside the machine—signals and communications in the U.S. Army, early social networking (co-leading marketing at FriendFinder, launching LiveVideo with MySpace co-founder Brad Greenspan)—then walked away to build systems that keep people’s data, voices, and lives their own.

His work sits at the convergence of freestyle, sovereignty, and consciousness science. He’s advised tech giants and entrepreneurs, farmed and regenerated soil, and built security stacks and verses in the same sitting. Unbowed and Unbroken (2026) was his first framework; The Field Remembers is the book.
